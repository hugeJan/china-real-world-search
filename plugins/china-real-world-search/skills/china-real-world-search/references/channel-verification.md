# Channel Verification for Public Services

Read this file only after the main Skill's scope gate passes.

A government, public-institution, or SOE name does not make every related app, product, or technical question in scope. This protocol applies only to a channel used to complete an in-scope mainland-China public-service task.

## 1. Separate the propositions

For channel X, ask:

1. **Existence** — does the exact channel exist?
2. **Identity** — who operates or publishes it?
3. **Current usability** — is the relevant function usable now?
4. **Compatibility** — does it complete the target public-service process?
5. **Official relationship** — what relationship is actually established?
6. **Locality** — does the evidence apply to the user's jurisdiction?
7. **Time** — current, recent, historical, unavailable, or unknown?

Never let one proposition silently prove another.

## 2. Official-relationship labels

Use only a supported label:

- **official self-operated** — the responsible authority, institution, operator, or clearly identified official technical entity operates it;
- **official-platform integrated** — the service appears inside an official platform but may be technically supplied by another entity;
- **officially named/linked** — a competent current source names or links it for the relevant task;
- **officially recommended/designated** — a competent current source explicitly gives it that stronger status;
- **no official relationship established** — none of the above is proven.

These labels do not automatically rank usability.

Do not upgrade:

- `可通过 X 办理` → `指定 X`;
- `官方平台里有 X` → `X 是官方自营`;
- `能用` → `官方认可`;
- `主体是机关/事业单位` → `该具体功能当前一定获授权`.

## 3. Practical labels

Keep compatibility separate:

- **compatible third-party** — evidence supports acceptance by the target process;
- **practically reported** — recent use is reported but acceptance evidence is incomplete;
- **provider self-claim only** — only the provider claims officiality or compatibility;
- **unknown** — evidence is insufficient.

Keep current usability separate:

- confirmed usable now;
- recently evidenced usable;
- historical only;
- currently unavailable;
- unknown.

## 4. Evidence roles

Useful discovery sources:

- ordinary web search;
- public provider pages;
- app-store or issuer identity pages;
- platform-native search when accessible;
- maps and recent community reports.

Useful proof sources:

- competent authority or operator guide;
- current institution page/account;
- official service directory;
- current target-process acceptance evidence;
- official migration or operational notice.

A weak source can discover a name. It should not define a statutory requirement or prove its own marketing claim.

## 5. Historical official channels

When an old official source documents a channel:

1. record its date;
2. state exactly what function and relationship it proved then;
3. search current guides and migration/change notices;
4. inspect live state only if the host has access;
5. keep historical official status and current usability separate.

Correct:

`A 2023 notice confirms historical integration; current availability remains separately unverified.`

Incorrect:

`It was official in 2023, so it must work today.`

## 6. User firsthand evidence

Use the user's observation for the narrow proposition observed.

- `I opened X today` supports access/existence for that context.
- `I generated the receipt in X` supports generation, not target acceptance.
- `Office Y accepted it` supports compatibility for that place/time, not universal official designation.

Research only the remaining uncertainty.

## 7. Government-domain authorship

A provider name on a government domain is not automatically endorsement.

Distinguish:

- government-authored naming or recommendation;
- a citizen's question;
- a repost or quotation;
- an official response that is silent on the provider.

Read the exact government-authored statement that carries the relationship claim.

## 8. Identity and payment safety

Before recommending an unfamiliar channel:

- verify the exact name and legal subject when accessible;
- distinguish a technical vendor from the public institution it serves;
- prefer a verified institution entry over copied QR codes or unknown deep links;
- treat `官方指定`, `内部渠道`, `包过`, `100%通过`, and opaque/high agency fees as claims or risk signals;
- verify expected fee and payee before identity/payment steps;
- do not request unnecessary ID numbers, passwords, payment credentials, or sensitive screenshots;
- do not recommend unofficial APK mirrors.

Do not assume every third party is unsafe. Verify the specific relationship and flow.

## 9. No test transactions

Do not create a booking, application, account, payment, ticket purchase, or identity upload merely to prove a channel works.

A transaction already completed for the user's real task may be evidence. A new consequential action requires the user's explicit goal and the host's normal confirmation flow.

## 10. Recommendation rule

Recommend the path that best meets the user's objective without overstating provenance.

For convenience, a well-supported compatible third party may be best.

For minimum ambiguity, prefer an official self-operated/integrated/named path or the responsible counter.

State:

- what works;
- what is official;
- what is merely compatible;
- what is historical or unverified.
